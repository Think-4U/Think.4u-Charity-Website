Security Vulnerability Disclosure & Bug Bounty Policy

Responsible disclosure framework for security researchers, ethical hackers, and cybersecurity professionals testing https://think4u.org.

Responsible Cybersecurity Research

Think4U welcomes responsible security researchers, ethical hackers, and members of the cybersecurity community who identify and responsibly report vulnerabilities affecting https://think4u.org. We believe in coordinated disclosure to safeguard our donors, volunteers, and beneficiaries.

Submit Vulnerability Report

1 Purpose

This policy establishes a responsible vulnerability-reporting process and explains when recognition, digital appreciation certificates, or other rewards may be provided.

2 What Can Be Reported (In-Scope)

Website, authentication systems, authorization/access-control weaknesses, API endpoints, SQL/NoSQL injection, Cross-Site Scripting (XSS), Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF), Insecure Direct Object References (IDOR), security misconfigurations, sensitive information disclosure, payment-flow integrity bugs, session management vulnerabilities, and privilege escalation.

3 Responsible Testing Guidelines

  • Test only authorized Think4U production/staging domains (think4u.org).
  • Avoid accessing, reading, or storing real user/donor personal data.
  • Create and use your own test accounts for evaluation.
  • Avoid disruption, service degradation, or denial of service.
  • Do not introduce malicious persistence, malware, backdoors, or social engineering.
  • Do not execute fraudulent transactions or alter live donation records.
  • Stop testing immediately once proof of concept is established and report promptly.

4 Prohibited Testing (Strictly Out of Scope)

Unless explicitly authorized in writing by Think4U leadership, the following actions are strictly prohibited:

• Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS) attacks or harmful volume stress testing.

• Brute force attacks against legitimate donor accounts or staff logins.

• Credential stuffing, phishing, vishing, or social engineering targeting Think4U staff or volunteers.

• Physical attacks against facilities, offices, or personnel.

• Malware, trojans, ransomware, or web shells.

• Unauthorized downloading, copying, or public dissemination of donor or beneficiary data.

• Attacks directly against third-party providers (Cashfree, Google, Cloudflare, etc.).

5 Privacy of Other Users

If a vulnerability inadvertently exposes another person’s personal data, stop accessing it immediately, access only the minimum necessary evidence to establish proof, do not copy, download, publish, or share it, do not contact affected individuals, and securely delete inadvertently obtained data where legally permissible.

6 Reporting Process

Report all findings to info@think4u.org with the subject line [Vulnerability Report] Component - Vulnerability Type.

• Vulnerability Title & Severity (Low / Medium / High / Critical)

• Affected URL, API Endpoint, or Parameter

• Step-by-Step Reproduction Instructions

• Proof of Concept (Screenshots / Script / Request Payload)

• Demonstrated Impact & Suggested Remediation

Do NOT include passwords, OTPs, or credit card information in reports.

7 Responsible Disclosure & Embargo

Researchers must give Think4U reasonable time (typically up to 90 days from verified acknowledgment) to investigate, fix, and verify the remediation before making any public disclosure. Premature public disclosure without reasonable opportunity invalidates recognition and reward eligibility.

8 Bug Bounty and Recognition

Think4U may, at its sole discretion, provide digital appreciation certificates, vulnerability-discovery certificates, acknowledgement on an upcoming security Hall of Fame, appreciation letters, or professional recommendations. Recognition is entirely discretionary and not guaranteed for every report.

9 Monetary Bug Bounties

Unless a specific monetary bounty program is expressly published with defined tiers and funding, no monetary reward is guaranteed. Any future bounty program will specify explicit eligibility, scope, tiers, and payment conditions.

10 Certificate Eligibility

A digital certificate may be issued where Think4U determines the vulnerability is genuine, reproducible, within scope, responsibly disclosed, and of demonstrable impact. A certificate does not constitute employment, professional accreditation, government certification, or a guarantee of future engagements. Think4U reserves the right to verify researcher identity.

11 Duplicate Reports

For duplicate vulnerability reports, recognition goes to the researcher whose complete and actionable report was received first chronologically, subject to Think4U’s evaluation.

12 Out-of-Scope Reports

The following categories are considered out of scope and ineligible for certificates or bounties:

• Missing security headers (CSP, HSTS, X-Frame-Options) without demonstrated exploitability.

• Outdated banner versions without a demonstrable vulnerability.

• Theoretical or hypothetical issues without proof of concept.

• Self-XSS or attacks requiring unlikely user actions with zero security impact.

• Unvalidated automated vulnerability scanner output (e.g. Nessus, Qualys, Nikto dumps).

• Best-practice suggestions, cosmetic issues, or rate limiting observations without impact.

13 Safe-Harbour Intent

Think4U intends to encourage good-faith security research. Researchers following this policy in full, avoiding data access/damage/disruption, and reporting promptly will be treated as conducting authorized security research to the extent permitted by law. This safe harbour does not authorize unlawful activity, data theft, or testing outside the designated scope.

14 No Guarantee of Acceptance

Submission of a report does not guarantee acknowledgment, acceptance, remediation, recognition, a certificate, or monetary compensation.

15 Changes to Policy

Think4U may modify this policy as its cybersecurity program develops. Researchers should review the current version before conducting security assessments.
